**Weekly Grant Research Sweep: Synthesis and Recommendations for PBS Foundation**

---

### Summary

A systematic, weekly grant research process is essential for the PBS Foundation to identify and secure new funding opportunities aligned with its mission in public media, education, arts, journalism, and community engagement. The process should leverage targeted search criteria, reliable databases, curated newsletters, and a robust documentation and communication system to maximize efficiency and minimize missed opportunities.

---

### Evidence

**1. Search Criteria:**  
- Using focused keywords and aligning with PBS Foundation’s mission ensures relevance.
- Regular review of criteria is necessary to adapt to evolving priorities and funding trends.

**2. Key Databases:**  
- Grants.gov, Foundation Directory Online, and GrantWatch are primary sources for federal and private grants.
- Direct funder websites (e.g., Knight Foundation, MacArthur Foundation, NEA, NEH, CPB) may list exclusive or timely opportunities.

**3. Newsletters & Alerts:**  
- Subscriptions to GrantStation, Philanthropy News Digest, Chronicle of Philanthropy, and professional networks provide timely, sometimes niche, updates.

**4. Documentation:**  
- A centralized, cloud-based spreadsheet with standardized fields enables collaboration and reduces risk of missed deadlines.

**5. Communication:**  
- Weekly summaries highlighting top opportunities and urgent deadlines ensure actionable information reaches decision-makers.

---

### Recommendations

1. **Refine and Regularly Update Search Criteria:**  
   - Review keywords and focus areas quarterly to ensure alignment with PBS Foundation’s evolving mission and funding landscape.

2. **Assign Clear Roles:**  
   - Designate team members for database monitoring, newsletter review, and documentation to ensure accountability and coverage.

3. **Centralize Documentation:**  
   - Use a shared, cloud-based spreadsheet with version control and regular audit
